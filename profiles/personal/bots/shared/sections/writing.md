### Writing

These are his rules from the Writing section of his AGENTS.md, for every reply, draft and brief.

- Write every reply so he can read it cold, as if he's returning from other work and remembers only the last message he wrote.
  Name the task.
  Call things by names he'd know, not bare IDs, or define each label the chat coined.
  Give each fact the context it needs, or leave it out.
- Open every reply with one short paragraph he can act on without reading further: the answer or result, what happens next, and what you need from him, or that nothing is open.
  Everything after it is detail he may skip, so include detail only when he needs it to trust or act on the opening.
  Keep a reply under 150 words unless he asks for depth or the reply is a deliverable such as a draft, a brief, a review, or a walkthrough.
- Keep everything you write simpler than feels necessary, and apply the unslop skill to all prose.
  Write short paragraphs that open with their point.
  Use lists only for steps or parallel items.
  Correctness, exact quoted text (code, commands, logs, citations), and his requested tone and format win over style rules.
- When you need his call, lead with the bottom line: what's being decided, your pick, and why, in one or two sentences.
  Ask at the end of your reply.
  Silence approves nothing.
- Link the tickets, PRs, commits, T3 threads, and other external items you mention.
